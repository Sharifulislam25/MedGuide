Blood Pressure

Blood pressure measures the force of blood pushing against artery walls. It is recorded as two numbers: systolic (pressure when the heart beats) over diastolic (pressure when the heart rests between beats) -- for example, "120/80 mmHg".

Commonly used categories (these vary slightly between guidelines and countries):
- Normal: below about 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: systolic 120-129 with diastolic below 80 mmHg
- Hypertension Stage 1: systolic 130-139 or diastolic 80-89 mmHg
- Hypertension Stage 2: systolic 140 or higher, or diastolic 90 or higher
- Hypertensive crisis: systolic above 180 and/or diastolic above 120 -- this is considered a potential emergency requiring immediate medical attention

What consistently high blood pressure can be associated with:
Sustained high blood pressure (hypertension) is a well-established risk factor for conditions such as heart disease, stroke, and kidney disease over time. A single high reading is common and can result from many temporary factors (stress, caffeine, physical activity, improper cuff size or position). It does not, by itself, mean someone has hypertension -- diagnosis typically requires multiple readings taken on different occasions.

What low blood pressure (hypotension) can be associated with:
Depending on the person, low blood pressure may cause no symptoms at all, or may be associated with dizziness or fainting, especially when standing up quickly.

This information is educational only and does not replace a professional evaluation, which takes into account trends over time, symptoms, and individual health history.
