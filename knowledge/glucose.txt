Blood Glucose (Blood Sugar)

Blood glucose is the amount of sugar (glucose) circulating in the blood. The body uses glucose as its main energy source, and levels are regulated mainly by the hormone insulin.

Common ways glucose is measured:
- Fasting Blood Glucose: measured after not eating for at least 8 hours.
- Random (non-fasting) Blood Glucose: measured at any time of day.
- HbA1c (Hemoglobin A1c): reflects average blood glucose over roughly the past 2-3 months.

Commonly used reference ranges (these can vary slightly by lab):
- Normal fasting glucose: roughly 70-99 mg/dL
- Prediabetes (impaired fasting glucose): roughly 100-125 mg/dL
- Diabetes: fasting glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher, confirmed on more than one occasion

What a high fasting glucose result can be associated with:
A single elevated fasting glucose reading does not, by itself, confirm a diagnosis of diabetes or prediabetes. Diagnosis typically requires repeat testing, additional tests such as HbA1c or an oral glucose tolerance test, and evaluation by a healthcare professional who can consider symptoms, medical history, and other lab results together.

What low blood glucose (hypoglycemia) can be associated with:
Levels below roughly 70 mg/dL may be associated with symptoms such as shakiness, sweating, confusion, or dizziness. There are many possible causes, including medication effects, fasting, or underlying health conditions.

This information is educational only. Reference ranges vary between laboratories and individuals, and interpreting any specific result requires the full clinical context that only a qualified healthcare professional has access to.
