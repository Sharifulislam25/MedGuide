Hemoglobin

Hemoglobin is the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body, and carries carbon dioxide back to the lungs to be exhaled. A hemoglobin test is often part of a routine complete blood count (CBC).

Commonly used reference ranges (these vary by lab, age, and sex):
- Adult males: roughly 13.5-17.5 g/dL
- Adult females: roughly 12.0-15.5 g/dL
(Children and pregnant individuals typically have different reference ranges.)

What low hemoglobin (anemia) can be associated with:
Lower-than-typical hemoglobin may be associated with iron deficiency, vitamin deficiencies (such as B12 or folate), chronic disease, blood loss, or other underlying conditions. Symptoms can include fatigue, weakness, or shortness of breath, though many people with mildly low levels have no symptoms at all.

What high hemoglobin can be associated with:
Higher-than-typical hemoglobin can be associated with factors such as living at high altitude, smoking, dehydration, or certain underlying medical conditions.

A single hemoglobin result is only one piece of the picture. Determining the cause of an abnormal result typically requires additional tests and evaluation by a healthcare professional.

This information is educational only and is not a substitute for professional medical interpretation of an individual's results.
