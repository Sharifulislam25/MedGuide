Kidney Function Tests (KFT / BMP)

Kidney function tests measure how well the kidneys are filtering waste from the blood. Commonly reported values include:

- Creatinine: a waste product from normal muscle activity, filtered out by the kidneys
- BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen): a waste product from protein breakdown, also filtered by the kidneys
- eGFR (estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate): a calculated estimate of how well the kidneys are filtering, based on creatinine, age, and sex

Commonly used reference ranges for adults (these vary by lab and by muscle mass/age):
- Creatinine: roughly 0.6-1.3 mg/dL
- BUN: roughly 7-20 mg/dL
- eGFR: 90 or above is generally considered normal kidney function; lower values suggest reduced function, with sustained readings under 60 often used as a threshold of concern

What elevated creatinine/BUN or a low eGFR can be associated with:
Reduced kidney function can be associated with many causes, including dehydration, certain medications, high blood pressure, diabetes, or kidney disease. A single abnormal result is common and can be temporary (for example, from dehydration) -- confirming reduced kidney function typically requires repeat testing over time.

What low creatinine/BUN can be associated with:
Lower-than-typical values can be associated with lower muscle mass, pregnancy, or other factors, and are often not concerning on their own.

This information is educational only. Kidney function naturally varies with hydration, muscle mass, age, and many other factors, and should be interpreted by a qualified healthcare professional in the context of trends over time and other health information.
